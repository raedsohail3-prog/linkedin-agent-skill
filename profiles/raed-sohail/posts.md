# Raed Sohail: post templates

Posts written in Raed's voice (see `voice.md`). Use them as patterns for new posts: same length, same plain sentences, one question at the end, three hashtags.

Rules for every post:
- Tuesday to Thursday, 8:00 to 9:00 am Bahrain time. Stay 15 minutes after posting and reply to every comment.
- One true story or fact per post. No invented numbers, jobs or results.
- No em dashes, no hype words, no emoji walls. Run `/li-human` before publishing.
- Never published without an explicit yes to the exact text.

## Post 2: AI automation (published 2026-10-15, hook: Time Anchor)

```
Last weekend I let an AI agent clean my inbox. It moved 215 old emails to the trash in one session.

Some of them were from 2015.

Old login codes. Game newsletters. Welcome emails from apps I forgot I signed up for.

I didn't hand it everything. It showed me the plan first, I said no to 6 emails I wanted to keep, and it left those alone. Anything it deleted can still be restored for 30 days.

The same week, I connected Claude to my GitHub so it reviews every code change automatically and leaves comments, like a teammate would.

What I learned as a security student: the useful part isn't the AI. It's deciding what you let it touch, and keeping a way to undo it.

What's one boring task you'd trust an AI with, and one you never would?
```

## Post 3: Call center lesson (draft, not published)

```
I learned social engineering before I knew the word for it.

At my call center job, people called every day asking for help with their accounts. Most were real. You learn fast that the voice on the phone decides how much you share.

At university I found out this has a name. Social engineering. It's behind a huge share of real attacks, and no firewall stops a person who just gives the information away.

3 habits I kept from that job:

1. Verify first, help second. Even when the caller is in a hurry.
2. Urgency is a warning sign, not a reason to skip steps.
3. Be polite and still say no.

None of this needs expensive tools. It needs people who know what to look for.

If you work in security, what's the most convincing scam attempt you've seen?

#CyberSecurity #SocialEngineering #Bahrain
```
