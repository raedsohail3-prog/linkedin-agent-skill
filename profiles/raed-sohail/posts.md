## Post 3: Call center lesson (published 2026-10-20, hook: Confession)

```
I learned social engineering before I knew the word for it.

At my call center job, people called every day asking for help with their accounts. Most were real. You learn fast that the voice on the phone decides how much you share.

At university I found out this has a name. Social engineering. It's behind a huge share of real attacks, and no firewall stops a person who just gives the information away.

3 habits I kept from that job:

1. Verify first, help second. Even when the caller is in a hurry.
2. Urgency is a warning sign, not a reason to skip steps.
3. Be polite and still say no.

None of this needs expensive tools. It needs people who know what to look for.

If you work in security, what's the most convincing scam attempt you've seen?

#CyberSecurity #SocialEngineering #Bahrain
```

## Post 4: Security inbox habit (published 2026-11-03, hook: Personal audit)

```
Last week I read every security email in my inbox. Not the ads. The boring ones.

Here is what I found in just 7 days:

A phone number was removed from one of my social media accounts.
An app I didn't remember got access to my GitHub.
Several login codes arrived in one week.

Some of it was me. Some of it I had to check.

As a cybersecurity student, this surprised me. Every app already sends you a warning when something changes. We just treat those emails like spam.

So I made a simple habit:

1. Read every "new sign-in" or "password changed" email the same day.
2. If you don't recognise it, change the password and log out other devices.
3. Turn on two-step verification for every account that offers it.

It takes 5 minutes a day. Fixing a hacked account takes weeks.

When did you last check which devices are logged in to your accounts?

#CyberSecurity #InfoSec #Bahrain
```

## Post 5: Connected apps check (published 2026-11-11, hook: Time Anchor)

```
Yesterday Google sent me 3 security alerts in 4 minutes.

All of them said the same thing: "You allowed OpenAI access to some of your Google Account data."

It was me. I was signing in to an AI tool with my Google account.

But it made me stop and check. How many apps have I given access to over the years with one tap on "Sign in with Google"?

So I opened my Google account and looked at the list of connected apps. Some I use every day. Some I forgot existed.

As a cybersecurity student, this is the lesson I keep relearning. Every AI tool you connect gets a key to part of your life. That's fine, as long as you know which doors it opens and you take the key back when you stop using it.

It takes 2 minutes: Google Account, Security, Your connections to third-party apps.

When did you last check which apps can see your Google or Microsoft account?

#CyberSecurity #ArtificialIntelligence #Bahrain
```
